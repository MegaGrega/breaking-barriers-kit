How this event is won

You don't pick the problem. A nonprofit brings it, and the nonprofit is what wins: the UK edition announced "Bookmark Reading Charity selected as London winner," and winning charities get up to $200K in AWS credits plus a team to take the prototype toward deployment in their own AWS account.

Technical innovationVisible cleverness: grounded answers, tools, memory, voice.
Social impact potentialOne person's before/after, then the number at scale.
FeasibilityCould a small nonprofit run and afford this next month?
Presentation qualityVirtual, cross-city, time-boxed. The demo has to land.

What the judges praised in the UK winner: a reading tool that supported struggling children without shame. They praised its empathy and dignity, not just its technical quality. Build something the nonprofit rep will champion, and make one design choice that protects the end user's dignity.

Tools you'll be handed: temporary AWS accounts with Amazon Bedrock (Nova and Claude models), Bedrock AgentCore (runtime, memory, gateway), and Kiro. On Day 1, AWS runs workshops on agentic AI and AI-DLC, its spec-driven workflow for AI coding agents. Solutions Architects are on site all three days.

Standing out as the student

Most professionals on your team will show up with general experience. You can show up already set up: AgentCore tested, a starter repo, and skills that turn a messy session into a brief in minutes. Experienced developers respect software that's already running more than titles, so offer that.

Your 20-second intro

"I'm [name], a student at [school]. I build agents in Python and TypeScript,
and I've already run the AgentCore + Strands stack end to end, so I can have
a working agent on our nonprofit's problem by lunch tomorrow. I'm happy to
own the agent backend or the demo, whichever the team needs."

Moves that work

  • Be the scribe in the nonprofit deep-dive. Then post the brief within 30 minutes. Whoever writes the problem statement steers the team.
  • Talk to the nonprofit rep directly, and keep doing it. They're the person who wins. Show them concepts early and put their reaction in the brief.
  • Take a lane the demo depends on: the agent backend or the demo UI. If you built the demo, ask to drive it on Day 3.
  • Show progress as screenshots and links in the team channel, not as status updates.
  • Disagree with options, not opinions: "Two ways, here's the tradeoff, I'd pick A." If a senior picks B, commit and make B work.
  • Bring the Solutions Architects in early. Ask a sharp question on Day 1. They remember who did.
  • Volunteer for the post-event deployment team. That's months of real AWS work for a real nonprofit, with AWS people watching.
  • LinkedIn the same evening with a specific note: the nonprofit rep, your SA, and anyone whose lightning talk you liked.

Watch out: don't flood the team with long AI-generated docs. Keep every artifact to one screen. Use Claude Code to move faster and to keep the team aligned, not to produce volume.

Idea patterns

Most nonprofit problems take one of four shapes. Spot the shape in the deep-dive and you already know the stack.

Staff copilotGrounded Q&A over the org's own guides, with citations.
Intake & routingConversation → structured record → right program or person.
Personalised contentSame info, right language, reading level, format.
Matching & logisticsSupply ↔ need: food, volunteers, shelter beds.

Education

Shame-free reading buddy UK winner shape

A child reads aloud. The agent generates stories at their level about their interests, praises effort, and logs progress for volunteer tutors.

Nova Sonic (voice) · Bedrock · AgentCore Memory

Dignity: never shows a level or "behind" label to the child.

Tell it once UK winner shape

A young adult does one conversational intake. The agent builds a profile, matches training and job programs, and gives staff a warm handoff summary.

Strands tools (eligibility as code) · AgentCore Memory · DynamoDB

Dignity: no repeating your story to every caseworker.

Adviser copilot

Staff and volunteers ask policy or program questions and get answers grounded in the org's documents, with page citations and an "ask a supervisor" fallback.

Bedrock Knowledge Base (S3 Vectors) · Guardrails

Parent bridge

School or nonprofit messages are rewritten at the parent's reading level and in their language over SMS. Replies are translated back.

Bedrock · Amazon Translate · End User Messaging SMS

Health & food security

Benefits screener → ready-to-file

Plain-language questions check SNAP, WIC and Medicaid eligibility using deterministic rules, then output a document checklist and pre-filled forms.

Strands tools · Bedrock · Textract

Dignity: no account needed, explains why each question is asked.

Paperwork explainer

Photograph discharge or clinic instructions and get a plain-language, translated version with "call your nurse if…" escalation. It never diagnoses.

Textract · Bedrock · Guardrails (denied topics)

Surplus food matcher UK charity shape

Incoming donations are matched to partner pantries by need, expiry and distance, with a simple demand forecast.

Strands tools · Location Service · Bedrock

Caregiver companion UK charity shape

Remembers a caregiver's situation across visits and answers from curated, cited sources. It escalates to a nurse line.

AgentCore Memory (long-term) · Knowledge Base

Climate resilience

Personal preparedness plan

Takes an address plus household needs (meds, mobility, pets, language) and produces a plan, a go-bag checklist and nearby shelters. During heat waves it calls vulnerable residents to check in.

Bedrock · Location Service · Nova Sonic

Crisis message triage

Inbound texts during an event are classified by urgency, with location and needs extracted onto a map for volunteer dispatch. A human confirms every dispatch.

Bedrock · Strands tools · map UI

Aid intake from photos

Survivors photograph damage. A multimodal model drafts the aid application and asks only the questions still missing.

Nova (multimodal) · Bedrock · S3

Volunteer shift matcher

Matches volunteer skills and availability to disaster-response shifts and drafts the briefing for each shift.

Strands tools · DynamoDB · Bedrock
